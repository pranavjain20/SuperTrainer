# Day 2 Scratch — Pranav's Reflections (save immediately, fold into devlog)

## What Clicked (Q1)

The big breakthrough was breaking the illusion of the "black box." AI (Claude) is one brain, and all the things people talk about — tool use, RAG, fine-tuning, embeddings — are just different ways of using that brain. But even knowing that, it's confusing because you hear "when to use one vs another, how does this work vs that" and it all jumbles together in your head. Today was step one in distilling that — really making the concept clearer about how the different pieces play together. That part was very helpful.


## Can He Explain Tool Use? (Q3)

Pranav's explanation: Claude is trained to answer in sentences — that's its default. But depending on your task, you might not want sentences. For our parser, we need structured data (exercise cards, observation cards) — a table of exercises, not a paragraph. So tool_use lets you train/maneuver Claude to give answers in a format that's useful for you. It's not just skipping a layer you'd have to build yourself (like regex parsing) — it's also using Claude's intelligence to make that layer smarter. A hand-built parsing layer would be dumber AND take more time.

## The Speed vs Depth Tension (Pranav's words, captured in real-time)

Core tension: If he was working alone, he'd spend three weeks on one feature. He'd make wrong design decisions, wrong technical decisions, debug them, and learn deeply from the struggle. With AI, it all happens in a few days instead of weeks or months. The understanding feels different — faster but shallower.

This leads to an existential question about his CS education: the world is moving to a place where AI handles the coding. So what was the point? Is his CS degree just tuning him to think a certain way — to know which questions to ask? Someone without CS wouldn't ask "why two tools instead of one?" — not because they're dumb, but because they don't know the question exists.

The deeper question: is all this learning (tool_use, schema design, how Claude works under the hood) for his own intellectual development, or is it actually useful? Everything they're building together — is the human insight part actually irreplaceable, or is that getting automated too?

What Claude said that resonated: The questions Pranav asks come from understanding that the question CAN be asked. That's the CS education. The coding itself is getting commoditized, but the architecture decisions, the "what needs to be built," the real-world insights (like per-client weight units from his own trainer experience) — every single one of those insights is going into the product and can't be automated. The system they're building together is shaped by his real understanding.

Also important: they haven't reached the testing/breaking phase yet. The real deep understanding comes when you use the thing, it breaks, and you figure out why. Day 6 (real transcript testing) is where the parser will fail in unexpected ways, and debugging those failures is where bone-deep understanding forms. The struggle isn't gone — it's just ahead of them, not behind them.

Pranav's feeling: it's a weird phase to be in. Coming to terms with what matters in a world where the mechanics of coding are being commoditized, while simultaneously building something that clearly requires human judgment and domain insight at every step.
