# Day 2 — Feb 20, 2026

**Threw out the framing. Rebuilt the product direction from scratch.**

## The pivot

Yesterday I built a working v1 backend — 93 tests, 7 models, 15 endpoints. Today I stepped back and changed what I'm building and why.

The v1 PRD was framed around monetization: billing tiers, subscription flows, payment infrastructure baked into the architecture from day one. But that was optimizing for the wrong thing at the wrong stage. The new framing: SuperTrainer is a portfolio and research project first. A real product that demonstrates deep understanding of AI + fitness, with genuine utility for trainers. If trainers love it and it grows, monetization follows naturally. The goal for the next 2-2.5 months is to build something that real trainers use and find helpful — not something with a payment form.

This changed what features get prioritized, how the intelligence layer is designed, and what "done" means.

## The big data model change

The biggest structural shift: **ExerciseLog replaced by SessionEntry.**

In v1, a session was a list of exercises. But trainers don't think in exercises — they think in moments. "She did 4 sets of squats, then I noticed her knee tracking inward, then we switched to leg press." That's a timeline of mixed events: exercise cards and observation cards interleaved. SessionEntry is a unified model with `entry_type` (exercise_card | observation_card). Sessions become timelines, not exercise lists.

This touches every layer — models, schemas, routes, services, tests, seed data. No way to incrementally migrate, so I committed to a clean rebuild.

Other new models: SessionPlan (plans as first-class entities), BrainConversation + BrainMessage (persistent conversation history for the AI agent), and pgvector embedding columns on text-heavy fields for future semantic search.

## Feature reprioritization

- Billing/subscriptions pushed out of scope for initial build
- The Brain (conversational AI agent) promoted to core — trainer asks "how has Marcus's knee been trending?" and gets an intelligent, data-grounded answer
- Pre-session briefings redesigned with data maturity gates: new client gets observations, long-term client gets trend analysis and risk alerts. The AI doesn't overstate what the data supports.
- Voice pipeline changed from batch upload to per-clip real-time processing

## What I learned

- Working code in the wrong direction is harder to course-correct than no code. 93 passing tests created momentum, but the underlying model was wrong for what I actually want to build. Better to catch this on Day 2 than after 300 tests.
- Speccing everything before coding eliminates ambiguity. Tomorrow's session starts with every field, relationship, and endpoint defined. No design decisions left to make during implementation.
